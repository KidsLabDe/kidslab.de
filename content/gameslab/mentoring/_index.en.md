## How a workshop day works

<h3>8:30 am: Arrival</h3><p>Short briefing, check laptops, lay out materials. The class arrives at 9 am.</p><p><strong>Your role:</strong> arrive relaxed, quick chat with the other mentor.</p>

<h3>9:00 – 11:30 am: Workshop</h3><p>Students get to know Scratch and build their own games:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Technical help:</strong> when the code doesn't run</p></li><li><p><strong>Encourage:</strong> when frustration builds up</p></li><li><p><strong>Celebrate together:</strong> when it works</p></li><li><p><strong>Move around:</strong> make sure nobody gets left behind</p></li></ul>

<h3>11:30 am – 12:30 pm: Wrap-up</h3><p>Students show off their games and celebrate their successes. Then: tidy up and a short feedback round.</p><p><strong>Your role:</strong> clap, praise, marvel — and mean it. Then pack away the laptops together.</p>

## What you should bring

<h3>What matters:</h3><ul><li><p>Enthusiasm for working with young people</p></li><li><p>Patience — a lot of it</p></li><li><p>The ability to show enthusiasm</p></li><li><p>Reliability on the days you sign up for</p></li></ul>

<h3>What helps:</h3><ul><li><p>Basic knowledge of Scratch or another programming language</p></li><li><p>Experience with children or teenagers</p></li><li><p>Staying calm when 25 students shout "help!" at once</p></li></ul>

<h3>Not necessary:</h3><ul><li><p>A computer science degree or training</p></li><li><p>Prior experience as a mentor</p></li><li><p>Answers to every technical question</p></li><li><p>Pedagogical training</p></li></ul>

## Still hesitating?

<p><strong>"I can barely use Scratch myself"</strong><br>No problem. You'll get an introduction, and the basics take about an hour to learn. The students start from zero — you just need to be one step ahead.</p>

<p><strong>"I have no experience with kids"</strong><br>The teacher is there and knows the class. You're the technical support, not the pedagogical lead.</p>

<p><strong>"What if I can't answer a question?"</strong><br>Then you say: "Good question, let's figure it out together." That's exactly how programming works.</p>
