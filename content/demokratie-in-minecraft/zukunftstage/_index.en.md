> The evolution of the Future Nights: ten municipalities across Bavaria, project days at schools, discussion with local politics.

Since January 2026, we've been running future workshops at Bavarian schools as part of the **Digital Future Days**. The proven Future Nights concept has evolved: instead of running overnight, the workshops now take place during the day — supplemented by a classroom lesson, an extracurricular meeting, and a discussion event with local politicians.

## What's new compared to the Future Nights?

| | Future Nights (2022–2024) | Future Days (2025/26) |
|---|---|---|
| **Format** | Overnight at school | Project day (8am–4pm) |
| **Structure** | Two parts (night + discussion) | Four parts (day + lesson + meeting + discussion) |
| **School types** | Realschulen and Mittelschulen | Realschulen, Mittelschulen, and Wirtschaftsschulen |
| **Target group** | From grade 8 | From grade 8 |
| **Municipalities** | 27 schools across Bavaria | 10 selected municipalities |
| **Partners** | BLZ + KidsLab + Q3 | BLZ + BJR + KidsLab + Q3 |

## The four-part concept

### 1. Digital Future Day at the school

A whole school day (8 am–4 pm) is dedicated to the question: *What should our municipality look like in the future?*

After an input phase on the topics "How does local politics work?" and "How can ideas become reality?", students start their creative phase in [Minetest](https://www.minetest.net/). In teams, they develop visions — from youth centres to bike paths to solar panels — and build them as 3D models. At the end of the day, all groups present their results.

| Time | Content |
|---|---|
| 8:00–8:10 am | Welcome, introductions, warm-up |
| 8:10–8:40 am | Short workshop: how does local politics work? |
| 8:40–9:10 am | Future workshop: my municipality today and tomorrow |
| 9:10–9:20 am | Introduction to Minetest |
| 9:20–9:55 am | First part of the creative phase |
| 9:55–10:10 am | Break |
| 10:10–11:55 am | Creative phase in Minetest with feedback rounds |
| 11:55 am–1:00 pm | Lunch break |
| 1:00–3:15 pm | Independent work in Minetest |
| 3:15–3:45 pm | Presentation of results |
| 3:45–4:00 pm | Evaluation and outlook |

### 2. Classroom lesson on local politics

In a lesson developed by the BLZ, students deepen their understanding of political levels and political participation. The Bavarian State Centre provides teachers with all materials and the lesson plan.

### 3. Extracurricular preparation meeting

Students from different schools and classes within a municipality come together. Together with the [Bavarian Youth Council](https://www.bjr.de), they work on presentations and arguments to make their project ideas achievable. This event serves as direct preparation for the discussion event.

### 4. Discussion event with local politicians

Students present their visions of the future to representatives of the municipality, district, or region — for example at the town hall or district office. To maintain political neutrality, representatives of various parties and voter alliances are present. The goal: a **future contract** with concrete next steps.

## Why Future Days?

More and more young people view the future with concern. According to a representative survey by the Vodafone Foundation, **86% of 14- to 24-year-olds** say they're worried. Only **23%** believe Germany will get climate change under control by 2050. The study "Jugend in Deutschland 2025" shows: many teenagers feel that politics doesn't take them seriously.

The Future Days give teenagers a voice and bring their visions into local politics. What makes it special:

- **Low barrier to entry** — gaming as a bridge to civic education
- **Visualised concepts** instead of abstract wish lists
- **Grounded in real local places** — the students' own municipality takes centre stage
- **Real impact** — future contracts with concrete goals and timeframes
