Soldering is like gluing with metal — only way cooler! With a soldering iron and some solder, you can connect electronic components and build your own circuits. Sounds complicated? It really isn't! Here you'll learn the basics.

![Kids soldering](gemini_generated_image_6bcp2i6bcp2i6bcp.jpg)

## What is soldering, anyway?

When you solder, you heat a metal called **solder** with a **soldering iron** until it melts. The liquid solder flows between two metal parts and joins them as it solidifies again. That's how electrical connections are made — for example on a circuit board.

## What you need

- A **soldering iron** (ideally a soldering station with temperature control)
- **Solder** (thin wire on a spool)
- A **soldering iron stand** (so the hot iron stands safely)
- A **sponge or brass wool** (for cleaning the tip)
- A **circuit board or practice board** (for practising)
- **Components** to solder in (e.g. LEDs, resistors)

## Safety first!

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**Important:** A soldering iron gets hotter than 300 degrees! Follow these rules:
- **Never** touch the tip of the soldering iron
- Always put the soldering iron back in its **stand**
- Work in a **well-ventilated** room (don't breathe in the solder fumes)
- Wear **safety glasses**
- **Always have an adult** with you
{{< /callout >}}

## Step 1: Prepare the soldering iron

1. Plug in the soldering iron and wait for it to heat up (approx. 2–3 minutes)
2. Set the temperature to about **320–350 degrees**
3. Clean the tip on the damp sponge or the brass wool
4. **Tin the tip:** touch a bit of solder to the tip until it looks shiny silver — this is called "tinning"

## Step 2: Insert the component

1. Push your component (e.g. a resistor) through the holes in the circuit board
2. Bend the legs slightly apart on the back so the component doesn't fall out
3. Turn the board over — you always solder on the **back**

## Step 3: Your first solder joint

Now it gets real! Here's how to make a clean solder joint:

1. **Hold the soldering iron to the joint:** touch the component's leg and the copper pad on the board at the same time with the soldering iron tip
2. **Wait briefly** (about 2–3 seconds) — so both heat up
3. **Add solder:** touch the solder wire to the joint (not to the iron!). The solder should flow into the joint on its own
4. **Remove the solder**, then **remove the soldering iron**
5. **Don't move it!** Let the joint cool undisturbed

{{< callout type="info" >}}
**Tip:** The whole thing should only take **3–5 seconds**. If you heat it too long, you can damage the component!
{{< /callout >}}

## What does a good solder joint look like?

| Good solder joint | Bad solder joint |
|---|---|
| Shiny and silvery | Dull and grey |
| Cone-shaped (like a little volcano) | Ball-shaped (too much solder) |
| Smooth | Cracked or crumbly |

## Step 4: Trim the excess legs

If your solder joint looks good, trim the excess leg with **side cutters** just above the joint. Careful: the cut-off piece can fly off — don't forget your safety glasses!

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Cold joint** (dull, cracked): you soldered too briefly or not hot enough. Reheat and add a bit more solder.
- **Solder bridge** (two joints connected): too much solder! Use the soldering iron to remove the excess.
- **Component moves**: fix the legs better beforehand or hold it down with tape.

## Well done!

You've made your first solder joints — congratulations! Soldering is like riding a bike: a bit wobbly at first, but it gets better with practice. Why not try a small project next, like a blinking LED circuit?
